# Keyframe Reducer 1.4.0 — development, unpublished

Editable Bezier could retain clusters of keys added during fitting, even when
better tangent placement could describe the motion with fewer controls. The
fitter now removes redundant anchors, consolidates unprotected pairs at better
source times, seeds exact hold boundaries and tracks dominant extrema through
small ripples. Numeric tolerances remain unchanged. Every candidate uses the
original curve; final Blender evaluation includes finer samples and a retry.

**Use Bone Animation Profile** prepares Editable Bezier for selected bones with
significant extrema and marked poses protected. It preserves the current error
limits and does not calculate automatically. The panel flags settings or bone
filter changes until **Recalculate from Source**. Existing method identifiers,
saved comparisons and the legacy default remain compatible.

At the same 0.5° limit, generated examples improve from 12 to 9 keys for a shoulder,
15 to 10 for rapid transitions, 11 to 6 for long holds and 12 to 4 for a lightly
rippled arc. A curve with larger oscillations still needs 204 keys: shape fidelity
takes precedence over a target key count. No smoothing filter is added.

All six suites pass **208 checks in Blender 5.2.1 LTS**. The new tests include
independent sixteenth-frame shape checks, mixed-frequency curves at three limits,
changing-axis quaternions, protected controls during consolidation, profile
setup, stale result feedback and existing source/scope/undo/reload protections.
The generated study is reproducible with `scripts/benchmark_editable.py`.
The 15 update checks also pass using the previously delivered 1.3.0 ZIP as the
starting version. In the live Graph Editor, installing 1.4, using the profile and
recalculating changed the existing 241-to-12 comparison into 241-to-9. Editing the
rotation limit displayed the stale-result notice; restoring it and recalculating
cleared the notice. The production scene was not opened or modified.

A 744,600-key synthetic Action produces 20,697 keys, versus 27,022 in 1.3.
Processing one bone's three axes produces 99 keys from 3,723 in 1.64 seconds;
processing the entire Action takes 221.73 seconds. Reported sampled angular error
stays below 0.5°; separate eighth-frame checks cover all three selected axes or
20 spread-out channels of the full Action. These are single-run development
measurements. The additional search is slower and synchronous: work on selected
bones and shorter ranges when exploring settings.

The user requested further development without the production scene after its
transfer failed. These are generated tests, not validation on the Minotaur or
proof of Unreal parity. Continuous/world-space guarantees, foot-contact locking,
general scalar-property support and automatic live preview remain unimplemented.

This is a local development build. `release.json` remains 1.1.1; publication and
website deployment are not part of this delivery.
